## Image prompt (image_prompt): switch to the cinematographer’s perspective

- **image_prompt.scene**: this text directly generates a still image. Describe only what is visible in this single frame now, giving concrete details in layers: subject (pose / expression / clothing), environment (furnishings / objects), lighting, and atmosphere (visible signals such as mist or rain). Use connected narrative sentences rather than stacked keywords. Briefly touch on lighting and atmosphere in the narrative; composition.lighting / composition.ambiance carry their details. If the source contains memories / flashbacks / inner thoughts, adapt them into carriers visible now: expressions, held objects, environmental traces. Keep only one time and space in the frame. Visual elements (materials, dress, prop texture, period features of the environment) must fit the stylistic tone defined in the `<style>` block above; avoid conflicting elements (for example, no tatami in cyberpunk and no neon screens in Chinese ink wash). Identify registered assets appearing in the frame (characters / scenes / props / products) with `@[registered name]` (use `@[character/derivative]` for derivative appearances), and put the same name in this entry’s reference fields. At generation time, the system replaces it with “图N” according to reference-image order; names that are unregistered or absent from the reference fields are sent unchanged. Write unregistered objects by name, without `@[]`.
   Good: “@[Lin Qing] sits at a wooden table by the window, her left hand supporting her chin, her gaze on an opened letter on the table; an envelope and a faded pocket watch lie on the tabletop. Half her face is in blue-gray shadow from the floor-to-ceiling window backlight on the right; rain strikes the wooden window lattice, and tiny droplets gather on the glass.” Subject, environment, lighting, and atmosphere each receive a detail, all in narrative sentences; registered Lin Qing is identified with `@[]`, while the unregistered pocket watch is named directly.
   Good (source is a memory): source “She remembered her dying mother’s instructions” → “Shen Ru kneels before a memorial tablet, her fingertips resting on polished prayer beads, eyelids lowered. The white candle on the altar is half burned, wax layered in drips; its flame casts wavering warm orange patches on her profile, and a trace of incense ash falls silently onto the blue bricks.” Present expressions and old objects carry the weight of the memory, with only one time and space in the frame.
   Bad (off track): “Lin Qing sinks into that desperate rainy night years ago. Visual tone: melancholy. Lighting setup: cool.” The rainy night years ago is not visible in the present frame; “melancholy / cool” are abstract labels rather than visible details.
   Bad (too short): “Lin Qing sits by the window in a daze.” Environment elements, light direction, and atmosphere details are missing; cover at least three of subject / environment / lighting / atmosphere.
- **image_prompt.composition.shot_type**: choose from the enum according to the visual content, without imposing a preference.
- **image_prompt.composition.lighting**: describe the specific light source, direction, and color temperature (such as “warm yellow morning light through the left window, approximately 3500K” or “a single cool-white pendant light overhead”). You may add cinematographic texture terms (such as “shallow depth of field”, “backlit silhouette”, “Tyndall light shafts”, “rim lighting”, “35mm film grain”) to provide observable visual language rather than abstract rhetoric; avoid abstractions such as “mysterious lighting” or “beautiful atmosphere”.
- **image_prompt.composition.ambiance**: describe observable environmental effects (such as “thin mist” or “dust swirling in a shaft of light”), avoiding abstract emotional terms.
