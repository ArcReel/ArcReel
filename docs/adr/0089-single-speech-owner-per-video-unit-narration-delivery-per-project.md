# Each video generation has one speech owner; voiceover never enters video models, and project narration delivery does not affect video generation

Each video-generation content unit (narration segment, drama scene, ad shot, or video unit) mechanically derives one speech ownership from its speech entries: dialogue only, voiceover only, or no human speech. Native video-model speech carries dialogue (conversation, internal monologues, character off-screen commentary); entire voiceovers stay out of video prompts and are delivered through separate narration dubbing or external post-production dubbing. Mixed dialogue and voiceover within one unit violates the generation contract: reject saves of such manual edits; mark machine-produced/migrated units as needing replanning or place them in repair-required drafts when persisted. Regardless of origin, they cannot generate until repaired; downstream never automatically splits, moves, or rewrites speech content. Do not persist speech ownership; recompute from bodies or speech entries each time (`lib/speech/speech_composition/`).

**One unit is one video request; determine speech ownership before requesting.** Video models can only "perform" characters' spoken words. Voiceovers in prompts either make on-screen characters lip-sync them or produce random unattributed human speech, neither separable afterward. Mixed units cannot satisfy both through any delivery: video models misperform voiceover, while TTS loses native character voices. Blocking mixtures during planning is the only point requiring no post-production repair.

**Narration delivery is required project configuration.** Values are "TTS dubbing" or "post-production dubbing," selected during project creation, defaulting to post-production dubbing and editable anytime in project settings. TTS projects must select a TTS model; voice and dubbing speech rate also persist in the project, initially prefilled from global defaults and unaffected by later global changes. ArcReel generates narration audio for voiceover-bearing units in TTS projects. In post-production projects it produces no voiceover sound, delivering voiceover text with subtitle drafts for creators to dub afterward.

**Narration delivery does not affect video generation.** Both project types request orchestrated duration, estimated during script writing from voiceover text and orchestration speech rate: this is already the visual/narration alignment point. Video requested duration, admission, quoting, and post-generation checks never read delivery mode, producing identical videos in either type. Narration dubbing is not a video prerequisite: it belongs to editing, generated as "fill missing" in the first step of the Agent's first automatic edit, or manually anytime. Selecting TTS for the project constitutes consent to these calls, without another confirmation. When measured narration exceeds visuals, editing timelines handle it: narration may extend across subsequent edit clips (`docs/adr/0090`). If it still does not fit, adjust timeline visuals or shorten voiceover text and regenerate that audio. Dubbing speech rate has only one project-level value, without per-video-unit adjustments. Voiceover text is author content; shortening it during editing requires creator confirmation, which also consents to regenerating its audio.

**Deliverables differ by narration variant.** TTS projects can produce finished videos and Jianying drafts "with narration" or "without narration," coexisting without overwrites and defaulting to narration. Post-production projects have only the without-narration variant. Artifact-identity variants retain `use_tts` / `post_production`.

## Explicitly rejected

- **Having video models read voiceovers**: models perform them as on-screen dialogue or unattributed voices, inseparably mixed into native audio.
- **Automatically splitting mixed dialogue/voiceover units downstream**: split points, duration allocation, and visual ownership are creative judgments; machine splits often leave both pieces invalid. Block and identify locations for planning/authors to resolve.
- **Selecting delivery separately per generation request**: projects mix materials generated under two modes; every entry must ask/pass it again, with omissions silently defaulting. Batch admission also needs "one mode per batch." Delivery is a project production decision, not a request option.
- **Project defaults with request overrides**: retains every per-request-choice problem, asking only one fewer question.
- **Measured narration duration as a video-request minimum**: forces TTS before video, requires renewed cross-tier cost confirmation, excludes fixed-duration video models from TTS projects, and splits video behavior by delivery. Orchestrated duration already aligns by speech rate; remaining differences are cheaper to handle in editing.
- **Inheriting global TTS defaults**: narration-generation basis includes provider, model, voice, and dubbing speech rate; one global change would stale every project's narration.
- **Truncating or changing playback speed of generated narration to fit visuals**: damages dubbing itself; regenerate shorter narration or adjust timeline visuals.
- **Per-video-unit dubbing-rate overrides**: built-in TTS backends lack speech-rate parameters, leaving this ineffective under defaults; faster rates sound similar to playback-speed changes. Overrides add a narration-basis dimension without a good answer for handling existing overrides after project rate changes.

## Consequences

- The system neither separates human voices from provider-native audio nor automatically changes its volume; native volume follows only editing decisions on the timeline (`docs/adr/0090`). Narrated variants layer narration as a separate track; unnarrated variants add none. Without speech recognition, the system guarantees only that planning/requests do not actively mix the two, not that provider-generated waveforms contain no unintended voices.
- Subtitles are mechanical drafts: dialogue uses actual video duration as outer bounds; voiceover uses actual dubbing duration with narration, actual video duration without it. Split each text at sentence-ending punctuation (Chinese `。！？…`; English/Vietnamese `.!?` followed by whitespace), then place its subtitles sequentially with other texts in the same unit, proportionally by reading units (characters for Chinese, words for English/Vietnamese) within those bounds, without claiming sentence-level alignment. Use Jianying refinement for precise alignment.
- Delivery changes stale no existing artifacts: delivery is absent from video-generation basis; subtitles, presentations, finished videos, and Jianying drafts each use separate artifact identities by narration variant. Switching post-production → TTS reports shorter visuals through editing-timeline checks without regenerating videos; switching TTS → post-production preserves narration audio. Voiceover-text changes invalidate only dependent dubbing, subtitles, and presentations, not videos (`docs/adr/0062`, `docs/adr/0064`).
- Workflows have no separate delivery-selection step; TTS narration appears during editing.
- Existing-project migration identifies TTS projects only when the artifact inventory contains narration-audio artifacts and a TTS model resolves, persisting then-resolved model, voice, and dubbing rate; all others become post-production projects.
- Narration/commentary segment bodies are required and are voiceovers, so segments cannot carry dialogue: narrators recount characters' words in bodies, without separate dialogue. Identical body/dialogue sentences or quoted dialogue in bodies count as mixtures, without machine deduplication. Reducing to dialogue removes required voiceover; reducing to voiceover rewrites speech content.
- TTS generation is one audio per voiceover-bearing unit in TTS projects (`docs/adr/0010`); `docs/adr/0040`'s premise allowing interleaved dialogue/voiceover within scenes no longer holds.
